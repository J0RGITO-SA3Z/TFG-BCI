\chapter*{Abstract}

\section*{\tituloPortadaEngVal}

To date, brain-computer interface (BCI) systems have achieved reliable results through reactive paradigms that rely on constant external stimuli, which is not applicable to a dynamic system such as a video game.

Few studies have achieved results without external stimuli (via motor imagery, MI), and those that successfully manage it use hardware that lacks accessibility due to its high cost or low portability.

This work aims to design and implement a modular BCI system capable of processing EEG signals in real time and translating them into commands to control video games. The system utilizes a lightweight, affordable electroencephalograph alongside the MiRepNet deep learning model, adapted through \textit{fine-tuning} techniques to capture the specific characteristics of each user.

Experimental results show an average accuracy of 80% in the binary classification of motor imagery (right hand versus left hand) on recorded data (\textit{offline}), reaching values above 90% for subjects with high signal quality.

To validate the system in a dynamic context, two video games (\textit{Endless Wave Runner} and \textit{Project Arrows}) have been developed, integrating decision filters and assistance mechanisms, or \textit{``aimbots''}. These mechanisms help compensate for the variability of brain signals, achieving a seamless user experience and demonstrating that the mental control of complex applications is possible using accessible hardware.
